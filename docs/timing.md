# Timing and pacing

The electrical training ALLIANCE says most applicants find they have plenty of time to complete each test without rushing, and advises working steadily and carefully without spending too much time on any one question. That is the right mindset. The numbers below help you hold it under pressure.

## The budget

| Test | Items | Minutes | Average per item |
|---|---|---|---|
| Algebra and Functions | 33 | 46 | about 84 seconds |
| Reading Comprehension | 36 | 51 | about 85 seconds, including reading time |

Each test is timed on its own, with a short break between them. Time you save on algebra does not carry over to reading.

## A pacing plan for Algebra and Functions

Check the clock at three points. These checkpoints are our guidance, not official.

| Time used | Aim to have finished |
|---|---|
| 15 minutes | about 11 items |
| 30 minutes | about 22 items |
| 42 minutes | all 33 items, with 4 minutes to check skipped ones |

How to use it:

- **Do the quick types fast.** Substitution and pattern items often take under a minute. That buys time for factoring and inequality items.
- **Two-minute rule.** If an item has taken two minutes and you are not close, choose your best answer, mark the item on your scratch paper and move on.
- **Come back with fresh eyes.** Marked items are often easier the second time.

## A pacing plan for Reading Comprehension

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Read the passage once for shape | 2 to 3 minutes |
| Each question, going straight to the line | 45 to 60 seconds |
| Check at the halfway mark | About 25 minutes used, about half the questions done |

If you are behind at the halfway mark, stop rereading whole paragraphs. Go to the line the question points at, decide, and move on.

## Where time usually goes

- **Working in your head.** Without a calculator, mental arithmetic feels faster than writing, but it causes the errors that send you back. Write every step.
- **Rechecking items you already got right.** One check by substitution is enough.
- **Rereading a passage from the top for each question.** Note the topic of each paragraph in a word or two in the margin of your scratch paper, so you can jump back to the right place.
- **Fighting one hard item.** No item is worth more than another. Guess, mark and move.

## Practise against the clock

Untimed practice teaches the method. Timed practice teaches the pace. After you have worked through the section pages, do at least one full timed run of each test. The [free NJATC aptitude practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/njatc-aptitude-test?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=tv2v2s&utm_content=ibew-aptitude-test) is timed, so you can check your pacing against the plan above. Do it without a calculator, as on the day.

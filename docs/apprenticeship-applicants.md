# For union electrical apprenticeship applicants

If you are applying to an IBEW and NECA electrical apprenticeship, the aptitude test is one step in a longer process. This page puts it in context, so you can plan the whole application, not just the test day. The steps come from the electrical training ALLIANCE's application guide. Your local JATC runs the process and may add its own steps, so check its website too.

## The process from start to finish

| Step | What happens | What you can do now |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Application | You complete your local program's application form. It is checked against the program's basic requirements. | Get your transcripts and documents together early. |
| 2. Aptitude test | If you meet the requirements, you are scheduled for the NJATC aptitude test battery, if your program requires it. | Prepare with these notes. |
| 3. Results | About two to four weeks later, your JATC receives the results and tells you whether you qualified. | Keep a note of your test date. It sets your retest date if you need one. |
| 4. Interview | With a qualifying score, you are interviewed by a committee representing both NECA and the IBEW. | Prepare for it as seriously as the test. |
| 5. Ranking | Based on the interview and a review of your qualifications, you get an overall ranking. | |
| 6. Eligibility list | Your name stays on the list for two years. Openings are filled in ranking order. | If you are not selected in two years, you need to reapply. |

## Basic requirements to check before you apply

The ALLIANCE lists requirements that local JATCs may use. Your local program decides which apply:

- a minimum age
- one full year of high school algebra with a passing grade, or one post high school algebra course with a passing grade
- a high school diploma or GED, or in place of either, a two-year associate degree or higher
- official transcripts for high school and any education after it, and GED records if they apply to you

Some programs also require drug testing, a physical examination, a security background check or a valid driver's license.

The ALLIANCE says the minimum requirements can be waived if you have been working in the electrical construction industry and meet specific work-hour requirements, with documentation of those hours. Some programs also describe direct routes to the interview or to entry for applicants who meet set conditions. The Puget Sound Electrical JATC's registered standards, for example, refer to "Direct Interview" and "Direct Entry." Ask your JATC whether a route like this exists and what proof it needs.

## The algebra requirement and the test

The algebra requirement and the Algebra and Functions test go together. If your algebra course was years ago, expect the [expressions and equations](sections/expressions-and-equations.md) page to take the most work. Start with the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) to see where you stand. The ALLIANCE is clear that previous knowledge of electrical work is not required for the test.

## Plan your timing

- **The two-year list.** Ranking places you on an eligibility list for two years. Your position depends on your interview and qualifications as well as the test, so a strong showing at every step matters.
- **The retest wait.** If you do not qualify, the ALLIANCE's rule is a wait of at least three months (90 days) from your most recent test, and some programs set six months. See [retakes](scoring.md#retakes). Preparing properly the first time saves you months.
- **Missing the test.** If you cannot attend, contact your JATC to ask about another date. The ALLIANCE says rescheduling is not guaranteed.
- **Leaving early.** You cannot finish the tests on another day. If you leave, you are scored on what you completed, and you cannot retest for at least three months.

## If you need an accommodation

The ALLIANCE says it complies with the Americans with Disabilities Act. If you have a physical or mental impairment, including a learning disability, that may affect any part of the process, you must tell the JATC before, or as soon as, you are scheduled for the test. Requests made on test day cannot be handled that day. In most cases you need documentation of the disability and of the need for the specific accommodation.

## Practise before you apply

You may get only a few weeks between scheduling and the test. Work through the [7-day plan](study-plans.md#7-day-plan) before you submit your application, and take a timed run such as the [free NJATC aptitude practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/njatc-aptitude-test?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=ibew-aptitude-test).

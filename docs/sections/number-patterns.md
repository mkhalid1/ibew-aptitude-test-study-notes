# Number patterns

**In one line:** you see a list of numbers that follows a rule, and you pick the next number, a missing number, or a later term.

Number patterns are part of the **Algebra and Functions** test. The official sample page includes one, so expect to see this type on the day. They reward a short, fixed routine more than any other item type.

## What it measures

Whether you can spot the rule that links each number to the next, and then apply it. That is the same skill as reading a function from a table, which is why pattern items sit inside the algebra test. On the job, it is the habit of noticing how a measurement changes from one reading to the next.

## How the questions look

A row of four to seven numbers, then "What is the next number in the pattern?" with four choices. Variations ask for a missing number in the middle, or for a term far down the list, such as the 20th.

The rules you will meet most:

| Rule | Example (our own) | How to spot it |
|---|---|---|
| Add or subtract the same amount | 4, 10, 16, 22 | Differences are all equal |
| Multiply or divide by the same amount | 5, 10, 20, 40 | Numbers grow or shrink fast; ratios are equal |
| Differences that grow | 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 | Differences go 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| Squares or cubes | 1, 8, 27, 64 | Each term is 1³, 2³, 3³, 4³ |
| Two steps that alternate | 2, 5, 4, 7, 6 | Differences flip between two operations |
| Two patterns woven together | 1, 30, 2, 25, 3 | Every other number makes its own simple pattern |
| Each term from the two before | 2, 2, 4, 6, 10 | Each term is the sum of the previous two |

## A method that works

1. **Write the differences under the gaps.** Every time. It takes five seconds and solves most items on its own.
2. **If the differences are equal,** add the difference once more. Done.
3. **If the differences change steadily,** write the differences of the differences. A steady second row means a growing pattern.
4. **If the numbers grow or shrink fast,** try dividing each term by the one before. Equal ratios mean a multiply pattern.
5. **If the differences jump around,** split the list into odd positions and even positions. Two interleaved patterns, or two alternating steps, show up straight away.
6. **Check your rule against every number in the list,** not just the last two. A rule that fits three numbers but not the fourth is the wrong rule.

## Traps that cost points

- **Checking only the last gap.** The wrong choices are built to fit the last step under a different rule.
- **Missing a sign change.** In 40, 38, 34, 28, the differences are −2, −4, −6. The next difference is −8, not +8.
- **Splitting too late.** If two differences in a row have different signs, try odd and even positions before anything else.
- **Losing count of positions.** When the question asks for the next number in an interleaved pattern, work out whether that position is odd or even first.
- **Adding instead of multiplying for a far term.** For the 30th term of 2, 9, 16, 23, you add the difference 29 times, not 20, because the first term already counts as one.

## Timing

Most pattern items take under a minute with the differences written out. If the differences row, the ratios and the odd and even split all fail within about 90 seconds, choose the answer that fits the most recent step and move on. These items are easy to come back to once the rest of the test is done.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the NJATC aptitude test or any publisher's materials.

**Item 1.** What is the next number? 3, 7, 11, 15, ...

A. 18 · B. 19 · C. 21 · D. 23

**Item 2.** What is the next number? 50, 47, 41, 32, ...

A. 23 · B. 20 · C. 29 · D. 18

**Item 3.** What is the next number? 5, 20, 7, 18, 9, 16, ...

A. 11 · B. 14 · C. 13 · D. 10

**Item 4.** What is the next number? 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, ...

A. 18 · B. 20 · C. 21 · D. 26

**Item 5.** In the pattern 6, 11, 16, 21, ..., what is the 20th term?

A. 100 · B. 101 · C. 106 · D. 96

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: B (19).** The differences are all +4. 15 + 4 = 19.

**Item 2: B (20).** The differences are −3, −6, −9. They grow by 3 each time, so the next is −12. 32 − 12 = 20. *Why not A:* 23 repeats the last difference, −9.

**Item 3: A (11).** Split by position. Odd positions: 5, 7, 9, rising by 2. Even positions: 20, 18, 16, falling by 2. The next number is the 7th, an odd position, so it is 9 + 2 = 11. *Why not B:* 14 continues the even-position pattern, which is the 8th number, not the 7th.

**Item 4: C (21).** Each term is the sum of the two before it: 5 + 8 = 13, so 8 + 13 = 21. *Why not A:* 18 adds the last difference, 5, again. Check that rule against the start of the list: 1 to 2 to 3 does not add 5.

**Item 5: B (101).** The difference is 5. The 1st term is 6, so the 20th term is 6 plus 19 steps of 5: 6 + 95 = 101. As a rule: term n = 5n + 1. Check: n = 1 gives 6. *Why not C:* 106 adds 20 steps instead of 19.

</details>

# Expressions and equations

**In one line:** you solve for an unknown, multiply out brackets, factor, and simplify, all by hand.

This is the core of the **Algebra and Functions** test. The official sample page includes an item that asks which expanded formula is equivalent to one written with brackets, and the electrical training ALLIANCE asks applicants to show at least one full year of high school algebra (or a post high school algebra course) with a passing grade. That year of algebra is roughly what this page covers.

## What it measures

Fluency with the moves of first-year algebra:

- **solving** linear equations, including ones with the unknown on both sides, brackets and fractions
- **expanding** products of brackets
- **factoring** simple quadratics and differences of squares
- **simplifying** by collecting like terms and using the rules of exponents
- solving a pair of **simultaneous equations**

None of these need a formula sheet. They need practice until each move is automatic, because you have no calculator to check yourself.

## How the questions look

An equation or expression, then four choices labelled A to D. Typical stems, in our own words:

| Type | Typical stem |
|---|---|
| Solve | "If 5x − 9 = 2x + 12, what is x?" |
| Expand | "Which expression is equal to (2x − 3)(x + 5)?" |
| Factor | "Which is a factored form of x² − 9x + 20?" |
| Simplify | "Which is equal to 3(2a − b) − 2(a − 4b)?" |
| Two equations | "If x + y = 14 and x − y = 4, what is y?" |

## A method that works

1. **Name the type before you start.** Solve, expand, factor or simplify. Each has a different first move.
2. **Solve:** clear brackets, clear fractions by multiplying every term by the common denominator, get the unknown on one side, then divide.
3. **Expand:** multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second. For two brackets of two terms, that is four products. Then collect like terms.
4. **Factor:** for x² + bx + c, find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. Check by expanding your answer.
5. **Simplify:** expand first, then group the a terms, the b terms and the plain numbers.
6. **Check by substitution.** Put x = 1 or x = 2 into the question and into your chosen answer. If they give different numbers, your choice is wrong. This catches most expansion and factoring slips in under 20 seconds.

## Traps that cost points

- **Dropping the sign of the second term.** In −2(a − 4b), the −2 multiplies −4b to give +8b. Forgetting this is the most common wrong choice in simplify items.
- **Missing the middle term.** (2x − 3)(x + 5) has an x term from 2x × 5 and from −3 × x. Add both: 10x − 3x = 7x.
- **Factoring signs.** If c is positive and b is negative, both numbers are negative. If c is negative, the numbers have opposite signs.
- **Multiplying only one side of a fraction equation.** When you clear fractions, every term on both sides gets multiplied.
- **Adding exponents when you should multiply them.** x³ × x⁴ = x⁷ (add), but (x³)⁴ = x¹² (multiply).
- **Stopping at x when the question asks for y.** Reread the last line of the question before you pick.

## Timing

Budget about 60 to 90 seconds for most of these. Solving and simplifying are usually quicker. Factoring can take longer if you test pairs of numbers one by one, so list the factor pairs of c in a column and scan it. If a check by substitution takes longer than 30 seconds, skip it and move on: the method is a safety net, not a ritual.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the NJATC aptitude test or any publisher's materials. No calculator.

**Item 1.** If 5x − 9 = 2x + 12, what is x?

A. 1 · B. 3 · C. 7 · D. 21

**Item 2.** Which expression is equal to (2x − 3)(x + 5)?

A. 2x² + 7x − 15 · B. 2x² − 7x − 15 · C. 2x² + 13x − 15 · D. 2x² + 7x + 15

**Item 3.** Which is a factored form of x² − 9x + 20?

A. (x + 4)(x + 5) · B. (x − 4)(x − 5) · C. (x − 2)(x − 10) · D. (x + 4)(x − 5)

**Item 4.** Which expression is equal to 49 − 4m²?

A. (7 − 2m)² · B. (7 − 2m)(7 + 2m) · C. (7 − 4m)(7 + m) · D. (49 − 2m)(1 + 2m)

**Item 5.** Which expression is equal to (x³ × x⁴) ÷ x²?

A. x⁵ · B. x¹⁰ · C. x⁶ · D. x¹²

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: C (7).** Subtract 2x from both sides: 3x − 9 = 12. Add 9: 3x = 21. Divide by 3: x = 7. Check: 5 × 7 − 9 = 26 and 2 × 7 + 12 = 26. *Why not D:* 21 stops before dividing by 3.

**Item 2: A (2x² + 7x − 15).** Four products: 2x × x = 2x², 2x × 5 = 10x, −3 × x = −3x, −3 × 5 = −15. Collect: 2x² + 7x − 15. Check with x = 1: (2 − 3)(1 + 5) = −6, and 2 + 7 − 15 = −6. *Why not C:* 13x adds 10x and 3x, ignoring the minus on the 3.

**Item 3: B ((x − 4)(x − 5)).** You need two numbers that multiply to +20 and add to −9. Both must be negative: −4 and −5. *Why not C:* −2 and −10 multiply to 20 but add to −12. *Why not D:* +4 and −5 multiply to −20.

**Item 4: B ((7 − 2m)(7 + 2m)).** 49 is 7² and 4m² is (2m)². A difference of two squares, p² − q², factors as (p − q)(p + q). Check: (7 − 2m)(7 + 2m) = 49 + 14m − 14m − 4m² = 49 − 4m². *Why not A:* (7 − 2m)² = 49 − 28m + 4m², which has a middle term and a plus on the m².

**Item 5: A (x⁵).** When multiplying powers of the same letter, add the exponents: x³ × x⁴ = x⁷. When dividing, subtract: x⁷ ÷ x² = x⁵. *Why not D:* x¹² multiplies the exponents 3 and 4, which is the rule for a power of a power, not a product.

</details>

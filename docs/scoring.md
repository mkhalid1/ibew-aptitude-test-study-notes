# How scoring works

Short version: you get credit for each correct answer, wrong answers cost nothing, and you are told whether you qualified, not your exact score. The qualifying score is set by your local program, not by one national rule. Two programs that publish theirs are shown below.

## Raw score

The electrical training ALLIANCE says there are no penalties for guessing and that your score is based on the number of items you answer correctly. So:

- **Never leave an item blank.** A guess can only help.
- **If you run out of time,** fill in an answer for every item you have not reached. If you can rule out one or two choices first, even better.

The ALLIANCE also says that if you do not complete all of the tests, your score is based on the questions you do complete, and you cannot finish the tests on another day.

## What you are told

According to the ALLIANCE, you will be informed whether or not you received a qualifying score, and exact scores are not provided. Results reach your local JATC about two to four weeks after the test, and the JATC then tells you about your application.

So you may never see a number. The practical question is whether you cleared your program's bar, and by how much your result helps your overall ranking.

## The 1 to 9 scale and qualifying scores

The ALLIANCE's public pages do not publish a scale or a cut-off. Some local programs do. Two examples, checked live:

| Program | What its own page says |
|---|---|
| [IBEW Local 81](https://ibew81.org/content/aptitude-test) | Applicants are scored on a scale of 1 to 9. A score of four (4) or higher qualifies for an interview. Below four, the applicant waits 3 months and submits a new application to retest. |
| [Puget Sound Electrical JATC](https://www.lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/apprenticeship/_docs/0134.pdf) (Washington State registered standards) | Inside Wireman applicants must score five (5) or higher, and those who do not may not retest for six (6) months. Limited Energy (Sound and Communications) applicants must score four (4) or higher. Residential Wireman scores are used for counseling only. |

Neither page explains how the 1 to 9 scale is built. A nine-point scale with 5 in the middle is usually a "stanine," which places your raw score against a large group of other test takers. If that is how your program's scale works, a 4 or 5 means around the middle of that group, not 40% or 50% correct.

What this means for you:

- **The bar differs by program and by classification.** Puget Sound sets a higher bar for Inside Wireman than for Limited Energy.
- **Clearing the bar is not the finish line.** After the interview, programs rank applicants, and openings go in rank order. A stronger test result can help, depending on how your program weights it.
- **Ask your JATC.** It is the only reliable source for your program's qualifying score, how it ranks, and how long its retest wait is.

## Retakes

The ALLIANCE's rule, in its own words:

- "A full three (3) months (90 days) must elapse before you may retake the test."
- "The six month rule remains in effect after each subsequent retake of the test."
- If you retest before the three months have passed, your score will not be valid, and you will not be allowed to retest for another three months.
- Willful attempts to violate the rule may result in permanent disqualification.

The first and second lines can read as two different waits: three months after your first test, and possibly six months after later retakes. The page does not explain this further, and some local programs set six months from the start (Puget Sound, above). Before you book a retest, ask your JATC which wait applies to you.

## What counts as a good result

Nobody outside your program can tell you its cut-off unless it publishes one. Numbers quoted on forums and prep sites are not official.

Our study guidance, not an official standard: if you can get about 80% or more of the items right on well-made timed practice, without a calculator, you have a margin for nerves and harder items on the day. Aim to be solid on both tests, because Reading Comprehension is half of the battery.

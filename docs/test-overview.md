# The IBEW aptitude test explained

The test most people call the "IBEW aptitude test" is officially the **NJATC aptitude test battery**. It is used to select apprentices for electrical apprenticeship programs run jointly by the IBEW (International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers) and NECA (National Electrical Contractors Association). The tests are developed by the electrical training ALLIANCE, which was previously called the NJATC. Your local JATC (joint apprenticeship and training committee) gives the test and uses the result.

Every fact on this page comes from the electrical training ALLIANCE's own pages, or from a named local program's own page, listed in [Sources](sources.md).

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Official name | NJATC aptitude test battery |
| Developed by | electrical training ALLIANCE (previously called NJATC) |
| Used by | IBEW and NECA electrical apprenticeship programs, through local JATCs |
| Tests | Algebra and Functions, then Reading Comprehension |
| Algebra and Functions | 33 items, 46 minutes |
| Reading Comprehension | 36 items, 51 minutes |
| Total | About two and a half hours for the battery. Plan on about 3 hours at the test center. |
| Break | A short break between the two tests |
| Calculator | Not allowed. A watch with a built-in calculator must be removed. |
| What to bring | Photo ID. Pencils and all other materials are provided. |
| Guessing | No penalty. Your score is based on the number of items you answer correctly. |
| Results | Sent to your local JATC about two to four weeks after the test |
| What you are told | Whether or not you received a qualifying score. Exact scores are not provided. |
| Retakes | Not until three months (90 days) after your most recent test date. See [retakes](scoring.md#retakes) for the full rule. |
| Electrical knowledge | Not required |

Source: [electrical training ALLIANCE, Applying for Apprenticeship](https://www.electricaltrainingalliance.org/SamplePage/ApplyingforApprenticeship), checked 24 November 2026.

## The two tests

**Algebra and Functions** tests your ability to solve problems using algebra. The official sample items cover putting values into a formula, finding an equivalent expanded formula, continuing a number pattern, judging which statement about a formula is true, and matching a rule to a table of values. These notes split that content into four practice pages:

| Practice page | What it covers |
|---|---|
| [Formulas and substitution](sections/formulas-and-substitution.md) | Evaluating and rearranging formulas |
| [Expressions and equations](sections/expressions-and-equations.md) | Solving, expanding, factoring, simplifying |
| [Number patterns](sections/number-patterns.md) | Next term, missing term, far term |
| [Functions, tables and inequalities](sections/functions-tables-and-inequalities.md) | Rules from tables, inequalities, points, slope, proportion |

**Reading Comprehension** measures your ability to obtain information from written passages. You read a passage and answer several questions about it. The electrical training ALLIANCE notes that its published sample passage is shorter than those on the real test. Practice: [Reading comprehension](sections/reading-comprehension.md).

All items are multiple choice with one correct answer. The official samples show four choices, labelled A to D.

## Who takes it and why

You take the battery after your application has been checked against your local program's basic requirements. The electrical training ALLIANCE lists requirements that local JATCs may use, including a minimum age, one full year of high school algebra with a passing grade (or one post high school algebra course), a high school diploma, GED or two-year associate degree, and official transcripts. Some programs add drug testing, a physical examination, a background check or a valid driver's license.

The ALLIANCE says many programs receive four, five, six or more times as many applicants as they have openings. The test is how a program decides, on objective and job-related grounds, who moves on to the interview. It is not a judgment of your ability in other careers. The ALLIANCE says so directly.

The [guide for apprenticeship applicants](apprenticeship-applicants.md) walks through the whole process, from application to the ranked eligibility list.

## What happens after the test

1. Your local JATC receives the results about two to four weeks later and tells you the outcome.
2. If you obtain a qualifying score, you are scheduled for an **oral interview** with a committee representing both NECA and the IBEW.
3. Based on the interview and a review of your qualifications, you receive an **overall ranking**.
4. Your name goes on an **eligibility list for two years**. As positions open, names are taken from the list in order of ranking.
5. If you are not selected within those two years, you need to reapply.

## How to prepare

The ALLIANCE's own advice is to review the sample questions, and if certain types are difficult, to review similar material. It also says there is no need to memorize formulas or factual material. That fits how these notes are built: learn a method for each item type, practise it without a calculator, then practise against the clock.

1. Take the [15-minute diagnostic](diagnostic.md) to find your weak areas.
2. Work through those practice pages first.
3. Follow the [7-day plan](study-plans.md#7-day-plan), or the [2-day plan](study-plans.md#2-day-plan) if your test date is close.
4. Do at least one full timed run, such as the [free NJATC aptitude practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/njatc-aptitude-test?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=tv2v2s&utm_content=ibew-aptitude-test).

## Is everyone's test the same?

The battery and the rules on this page come from the electrical training ALLIANCE and apply to programs that use it. Local programs still set some things themselves: the qualifying score, test dates, how often they test, and some details of the process. One local's page, for example, says it tests monthly and mails applicants their test date. Always check your own JATC's page or call them.

# Frequently asked questions

These are the questions people search for most about the IBEW aptitude test, drawn from Google's "People also ask" results for US searches in November 2026. Answers stick to what the electrical training ALLIANCE and local programs state, and say so when the answer is our guidance.

## What does IBEW stand for?

International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers. Its apprenticeship programs are run jointly with NECA, the National Electrical Contractors Association, through local JATCs (joint apprenticeship and training committees). The training arm they share is the electrical training ALLIANCE, previously called the NJATC.

## Is the NJATC test the same as the IBEW aptitude test?

Yes. "IBEW aptitude test" is the everyday name. The electrical training ALLIANCE calls it the NJATC aptitude test battery. See the [test overview](test-overview.md).

## How long does an IBEW aptitude test take?

About two and a half hours for the battery, according to the electrical training ALLIANCE: Algebra and Functions (33 items, 46 minutes) and Reading Comprehension (36 items, 51 minutes), with a short break between them. The ALLIANCE advises planning on about 3 hours at the test center, which allows for check-in and instructions.

## Can you use a calculator?

No. The ALLIANCE says you will not be able to use a calculator for the tests, and a watch with a built-in calculator must be removed. Practise without one. See [timing and pacing](timing.md).

## Do I need to know about electrical work?

No. The ALLIANCE says previous knowledge of electrical work is not required, and there is no need to memorize formulas or factual material to do well.

## How hard is it to pass the IBEW aptitude test?

The maths is roughly first-year high school algebra, which matches the ALLIANCE's requirement of one full year of algebra with a passing grade. What makes it hard for many applicants is doing it by hand, under time, years after they last studied algebra. The reading half is often underrated. Our guidance: if you score about 80% or better on timed practice without a calculator, you are in good shape. Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) to see where you stand.

## Is it hard to pass an aptitude test?

It depends on preparation more than talent. Aptitude tests reward familiarity with the question types, a method for each, and practice under time. Each practice page in these notes gives you all three for one type.

## What's a good score on the aptitude test?

There is no single answer, because each local program sets its own qualifying score. Two published examples: IBEW Local 81 says applicants are scored 1 to 9 and need 4 or higher for an interview. The Puget Sound Electrical JATC's standards require 5 or higher for Inside Wireman and 4 or higher for Limited Energy. Above the bar, a higher result can help your ranking, depending on how your program weights the test. See [how scoring works](scoring.md).

## Will I find out my score?

Usually not the exact number. The ALLIANCE says you will be told whether or not you received a qualifying score, and exact scores are not provided.

## What happens if I fail my IBEW aptitude test?

You can take it again after a waiting period. The ALLIANCE's rule is that three months (90 days) must pass from your most recent test date, and it also says "the six month rule remains in effect after each subsequent retake." Some programs set six months from the start. A retest taken too early is not valid and adds another three months. Ask your JATC which wait applies. See [retakes](scoring.md#retakes).

## How many people fail the IBEW aptitude test?

No official pass rate is published. Figures you may see online are not from the ALLIANCE. What the ALLIANCE does say is that many programs receive four, five, six or more times as many applicants as they have openings, which is why the test is used to decide who moves on to the interview.

## How can I pass my aptitude test?

1. Find your weak areas with the [15-minute diagnostic](diagnostic.md).
2. Learn the method for each item type on its practice page.
3. Practise without a calculator, writing every step.
4. Do at least one full timed run.
5. Answer every item on the day. There is no penalty for guessing.

The [study plans](study-plans.md) put this into a day-by-day schedule.

## Is IBEW hard to get into?

It can be competitive. The ALLIANCE says many programs receive several times more applicants than openings. Getting in depends on meeting the basic requirements, qualifying on the test, the interview with the NECA and IBEW committee, and your overall ranking on a two-year eligibility list. See the [guide for apprenticeship applicants](apprenticeship-applicants.md).

## What should I bring on test day?

A photo ID. The ALLIANCE says pencils and all other materials are provided, and that no ID means no test. See the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md).

## Where can I practise for free?

- The electrical training ALLIANCE's [official sample page](https://www.electricaltrainingalliance.org/SamplePage/PreparingfortheTest) has a handful of sample items with an answer key.
- The 34 original items in these notes, each with a full explanation.
- The [free NJATC aptitude practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/njatc-aptitude-test?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=ibew-aptitude-test), which is timed.
